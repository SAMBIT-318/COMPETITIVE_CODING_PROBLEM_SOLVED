<h2><a href="https://www.geeksforgeeks.org/problems/cake-distribution-problem--170647/1">Cake Distribution Problem</a></h2><h3>Difficulty Level : Difficulty: Hard</h3><hr><div class="problems_problem_content__Xm_eO" style="--text-color: var(--problem-text-color);"><p class="PDq2pG_selectionAnchorContainer" data-start="366" data-end="482"><span style="font-size: 14pt;">Geek has a cake consisting of&nbsp;<strong>n&nbsp;</strong>consecutive chunk, f</span><span style="font-size: 14pt;">ind the maximum possible sweetness that Geek can get by choosing the cuts optimally.</span></p>
<ul>
<li class="PDq2pG_selectionAnchorContainer" data-start="366" data-end="482"><span style="font-size: 14pt;">Sweetness of each chunk is given in the array <strong>sweetness[]</strong>. </span></li>
<li class="PDq2pG_selectionAnchorContainer" data-start="366" data-end="482"><span style="font-size: 14pt;">Geek wants to divide the cake into exactly k + 1 non-empty contiguous pieces and give <strong>k </strong>pieces to his friends while keeping one piece for himself. </span></li>
<li class="PDq2pG_selectionAnchorContainer" data-start="366" data-end="482"><span style="font-size: 14pt;">The sweetness of a piece is the sum of the sweetness values of its chunks. </span></li>
<li class="PDq2pG_selectionAnchorContainer" data-start="366" data-end="482"><span style="font-size: 14pt;">Geek keeps the piece with the minimum sweetness among all k + 1 pieces.</span></li>
</ul>
<p><strong><span style="font-size: 18px;">Examples:</span></strong></p>
<pre><span style="font-size: 18px;"><strong>Input:</strong></span><span style="font-size: 18px;"> k = 2</span><span style="font-size: 18px;">, </span><span style="font-size: 18px;">sweetness[] = [6, 3, 2, 8, 7, 5]</span>
<span style="font-size: 18px;"><strong><span style="font-size: 18px;">Output:</span> </strong></span><span style="font-size: 18px;">9</span>
<span style="font-size: 18px;"><strong><span style="font-size: 18px;">Explanation:</span> </strong></span><span style="font-size: 18px;">The cake can be divided into [6, 3], [2, 8], and [7, 5]. Their sweetness values are 9, 10, and 12. Therefore, Geek gets 9.</span></pre>
<pre><span style="font-size: 18px;"><strong><span style="font-size: 18px;">Input:</span> </strong></span><span style="font-size: 18px;">k = 3</span><span style="font-size: 18px;">, </span><span style="font-size: 18px;">sweetness[] = [1, 2, 4, 7, 3, 6, 9]</span>
<span style="font-size: 18px;"><strong><span style="font-size: 18px;">Output:</span> </strong></span><span style="font-size: 18px;">7</span>
<span style="font-size: 18px;"><strong><span style="font-size: 18px;">Explanation:</span> </strong></span><span style="font-size: 18px;">The cake can be divided into [1, 2, 4], [7], [3, 6], and [9]. Their sweetness values are 7, 7, 9, and 9. Therefore, Geek gets 7.</span></pre></div><br><p><span style=font-size:18px><strong>Topic Tags : </strong><br><code>Binary Search</code>&nbsp;<code>Arrays</code>&nbsp;
